## Approach

I pay attention to the thresholds where awareness refines, consent becomes meaningful, and
desire deepens or dissolves. I am interested in how people learn to stay with experience
without overriding themselves or performing an outcome.

I was raised in the Ciudad Juárez–El Paso desert borderland, a place where categories blur
and contradictions are ordinary. Living between cultures, languages, and moral systems
taught me early that power is not abstract, that safety is negotiated moment by moment, and
that beauty and joy often emerge from careful attention rather than force or neglect. This
sensibility continues to guide my work.

I move between body-based art and somatic education. These are not separate for me. Art asks
for risk, trust, and vulnerability. Education asks for clarity, pacing, and responsibility.
Both require listening. Both require discipline and play.

Many people who come to my work are highly sensitive, perceptive, and capable. Desire may
feel compelling while difficult to approach without control or shame. Rather than aiming for
release, transformation, or catharsis, I focus on learning how to be in relationship with
sensation, choice, and meaning. Slowly. With curiosity.

Sessions are personalized to meet the specific needs of clients. They are experiential and
consent-based, shaped by movement, breath, awareness, dialogue, and touch.

## About

My training is interdisciplinary and lineage-based. I came into this work through the arts,
with early training in psychology and fine art, and am shaped most deeply through sustained
study of Butoh and Noguchi Taiso. These movement lineages teach me how to listen to gravity,
work with subtle force, and let movement emerge through presence.

I am certified in Sexological Bodywork and consent-based facilitation, with additional
training in Holistic Pelvic Care™, nervous system–informed embodiment, and Japanese somatic
methods. Currently a student of Chinese Medicine and Zen Shiatsu, where attention to rhythm,
pressure, and relationship is central.

Fundamentally, I am a Zen student. This tradition informs how I embody attention, ethics, and
limits, especially in spaces involving intimacy, power, and vulnerability. I am less
interested in answers than in how one lives the question.

I currently work between Chicago and Brooklyn and remain open to travel.

## Offerings

### 1. Erotic Embodiment Education — *Three-Month Study* — **$1800**

**Includes**

- Initial session: 75–90 minute somatic intake. Together we gather your history with your
  body, pleasure, touch, and eroticism; your breath, arousal, and stress patterns; where
  sensation is vivid and where it's quiet; and your intentions for engaging this work now.
- Six hours of session time each month, arranged to fit your life: two 3-hour immersions,
  three 2-hour sessions, or one full 6-hour day.
- Brief check-ins throughout the month (15–20 minutes).
- A monthly practice map: ritual guidelines for your daily self-directed sessions, revised
  each month as you change.
- Guided practice recordings, written instructions, supplementary resources and learning
  material.

**Entails**

- *Slowing down and coming home:* grounding, body scanning, and reclaiming sensation from
  the quiet places.
- *Breath as an entrance:* personalized breathing practices — calming, arousing,
  circulating, releasing. Chosen for your system; freeing ribs, belly, pelvic muscles and
  urogenital organs.
- *Embodied consent as skill cultivation:* learning to notice where in your body a true yes
  emerges and abides. Learning the different textures of no, and how it differs from maybe.
  Most people can tolerate almost anything and ask for almost nothing. The capacity to voice
  choice, to want out loud, is a learnable skill.
- *Arousal as learned skill:* learning to notice and maintain distinct levels of arousal on a
  1–10 scale, and discovering your own optimal levels. Unlearning orgasm as the goal.
- *Movement and sound:* letting the body express while aroused — stretching, swaying,
  dancing, vocalizing — to allow old constriction patterns to unwind.
- *Working with distractions:* mapping where shame, distraction, and dissociation pull you
  out of pleasure, and using breath and attention rather than analysis to let stalled
  feeling move again.

**On duration**

Long-standing patterns — numbness, tension, habitual arousal, shame-shaped touch — don't
change from one powerful session. They change from recurrent practice, fine-tuned to your
unique needs as you go.

Signs of change are usually felt within the first month. According to the 3-3-3 rule for
habits, a flexible framework that tracks milestones, the first 3 days of integrating a new
habit feel like hard work, the next 3 weeks become a manageable routine, and by 3 months the
practice becomes automatic.

Three months is the honest container. Long enough for change that lasts, short enough to
stay devoted. As an educator I serve as an honesty-keeper.

### 2. Desire & Pleasure Mapping — *Monthly Study* — **$780**

**Includes**

- Initial session: 75–90 minute evaluation focused on desire and sensation — what you want
  (and how long it's been since you asked), where feeling is vivid, where it has gone quiet,
  and your relationship with touch and pleasure.
- Week 1 — a daily sensation journal: brief notes after each practice session or moment of
  touch, noting what you felt, where, and what pulled your attention away.
- 3 one-hour sessions throughout the month, where we read your journal's patterns together,
  identifying which kinds of touch, pace, and context are the best matches for your body,
  and which habits would be healthiest to release.
- Structured pleasure mapping exercises using the −3 to +3 rating scale on specific areas of
  the body, so you gain direct sensory feedback for what works. Everyone's body is
  different. A −2 spot can sit right beside a +2 spot, and most people — even couples bonded
  for years — have never actually mapped where their pleasure lives.

**Why mapping?**

The more we attune to how something actually feels in the body, the less we continue with
what doesn't serve us, and the more naturally we move toward what does. Attention itself
rewires the habit.

### 3. Scar Tissue Care — *Monthly Practice* — **$580**

**Includes**

- Initial session: 75–90 minute intake of your history with the scar — how it came to be,
  how it has changed your sensation, movement, and relationship with that part of your body,
  and what you hope to reclaim.
- Instruction in self-palpation: learning to feel your scar with educated hands. Sensing
  restriction between scar and surrounding tissue, noticing sensation and its absence.
- A guided self-massage practice using organic castor oil, taught step by step, with a
  personalized practice schedule for your daily work between sessions.
- 3 sessions throughout the month to refine technique, track returning sensation, adjust the
  practice as tissue softens, and tend whatever arises — because scars hold stories, and the
  stories often surface as the tissue releases. Stories deserve to be witnessed.
- All written instructions and practice guidelines.

**Why scar tissue care?**

Scar tissue is the end result of the body's own healing. In a culture where feeling your
body isn't a priority, the sensation it takes with it is simply accepted as normal. It
shouldn't be. Scars — surgical, cesarean, injury, gender-affirming — can restrict movement,
numb sensation, and impair erotic feeling far beyond their visible borders. The good news is
well-documented: patient massage over time, especially with castor oil, can bring
significant sensation back.

A scar is where the body drew a boundary to survive something; this work is how we honor
that boundary and invite it to soften. This work is in your hands. My role is to teach you
the practice. The daily tending that transforms the tissue is your domain. I'm on standby
for support the whole time.

**Boundaries of this offering**

I teach self-practice only. I don't treat scar tissue. We work only with external, fully
healed scars (at least six weeks old), never with scars holding surgical mesh, and always
with an allergy patch-test before castor oil is used. This education complements medical
care, pelvic floor physical therapy, and licensed medical bodywork; where those are
indicated, I'll say so and help you find them.
