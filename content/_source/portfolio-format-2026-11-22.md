## About

Joan Gutiérrez develops tactile, performative, and participatory works rooted in the desert landscapes of the Mexican-American borderlands where she was raised. Born in 1992—the year the Ciudad Juárez femicides began and Dr. Judith Herman’s
Trauma and Recovery
was published—Gutiérrez situates her interdisciplinary practice within this charged socio-political terrain, engaging the body as a site of memory, resistance, and transformation: personal, collective, and planetary.
Rejecting the authority of specialization, Gutiérrez embraces the term
Amateur
as a radical, queered, and feminized position—one that privileges play, pleasure, and vulnerability as methodologies for transmuting rupture, both intimate and systemic.
Though trained in psychology at Loyola University Chicago and holding a BFA from the School of the Art Institute of Chicago (SAIC), Gutiérrez’s practice is shaped less by institutional frameworks than by sustained, embodied inquiry—studying independently with artists, somatic sex educators, and masters of Zen and butoh across Europe, Asia, North and South America.
She currently lives, works, and practices at the Zen Center of New York City – Fire Lotus Temple in Brooklyn.

## 15. Haircut Piece Part II
Format page: /work/haircut-piece-part-ii · about 46 images

Cut an onion crosswise and you get a model of the universe. Honor of divinity. As the blossoms begin to unfold  its structure can be seen clearly. On the outside are the modified leaf bases which during budding tightly enclose the whole flower and all of its delicate structures.Next are the petals, also modified leaves, colored in order to attract insects.The style with the stigma at its tip is dimly visible in the center of the flower. Soon the flower will be fully open and another life cycle can begin. Self-renewal from a leaf cutting like an African violet. In the coarse of a plant's life its roots twist and turn many times. sometimes growing upward, sometimes downward, the highly sophisticated reaction of the root to auxin may well be the answer to these erratic ramblings of the root.
So then, I think of you
getting mandolin-clink mixed with your hair - feeling your knees among the supercilious chimneys. Dress deftly your flesh in stupid stuff to phrase the immense weapon of your hair.
It's funny...you will be dead some day. By you, the mouth hair eyes and I mean the unique and nervously obscene - need - it's funny. They will all be dead. They are in me as in the hairy notching of clenched thighs. a friendless dingy female frenzy of bubbles. dead. Dark hair and nice hands with a little eye that rolled and made its print. in color. Hair. He put between her lips a moist mistake, whose fragrance hurls me into tears, as the dusty new-ness of her obsolete gaze begins to dye Hairy dead stars stink. Down. Inane, the poetic carcass of a girl.
The bright rile of jovial hair frames the face in a hoop of grim ecstasy Her hair cannot contain as the evil to feel. unwieldy...the blood beat. Her hair was mussed and she coughed while tying strings dead's more, even now how many ways of sitting on your head with your unnatural hair in the morning
You,  whose body is Egypt
whose hair is Nile
Scarlet goes / with her white hot / face gashed by hair's blue cold.
Skin like the poisonous fragility of ice newly formed upon an old pool. Her nose small.exact.stupid.mouth normal.large.un-clever. hair genuinely artificial, unpleasantly tremendous. Secrets she won't tell 'til you realize
The moon is hiding in her hair

## 18. #aself...
Format page: /work/aself · about 80 images

In the Spring of 2017, the artist announced an artistic intention to deactivate all social media accounts for an entire year. To prepare both followers and herself, Gutierréz posted a daily self-portrait under the hashtag #aselfieadaykeepstheinsecuritiesaway, exposing the filter used in each image. This pre-performance lasted a month, where the artist engaged in a ritualistic act of self-curation that reflected the pressures of online identity.
The performance formally began at 12 A.M. on March 6, 2017, with the deactivation of accounts. A year later, on March 6, 2018, Gutierréz had planned to present the findings of this experiment. However, instead of returning to social media, they encountered an unexpected resistance to re-engaging with the platforms.
What began as a year-long endurance performance transformed into a broader reflection on living more intentionally in the present. This piece evolved into a meditation on social media’s role in shaping cultural, social, and community dynamics, as well as issues of privacy, self-esteem, and identity.
