  arts:
    - My process bridges an East-meets-West sensibility, rooted in an eclectic set of contemporary somatic methods and ancient systems of medicine, philosophy, and internal arts.
    - I received my foundational training in Somatic Sex Education at the Pacific Center of Somatic Sexology, with continued mentorship from Ellen Heed, PhD, and Zen Shiatsu Chicago.
    - I come to this work from the arts, having studied at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago. An artist in all my strokes, I have been transitioning out of the fine art world since 2016 to practice Zen and teach Butoh across Mexico, the US, Germany, and Japan.
    - During that time, I aligned the heart of my teaching with Minako Seki’s pioneering evolution of Butoh. Practicing closely with her holistic view of the moving body, I began to see dance as an expression of living fully. The Seki Method emphasizes how we see, how we nourish ourselves, how we interact with our environment, and how we care for the body-mind before and after performance. It nurtures a wider, more inclusive, and interconnected view of what it means to live a creative life.
    - These artistic formations continue to lay the ground for my approach to health, creativity, and human potential.
  turn: My first and most profound teacher has been my own body.
  body:
    - A decade of hospitalizations, with experts making choices that altered the neurochemistry of my mind and the biochemistry of my body, followed years of surviving sexual and political violence on the feminized queer body. Shame became the shadow companion I never asked for, and my body and mind felt like strangers I learned to distrust.
    - Waking up with endometriosis in my mid-20s, after years of a muted menstrual cycle, taught me intimately about the connection between my inner emotional life, my ancestral inheritances, my diet and lifestyle, and the intensity of my pelvic pain. I worked extensively with professionals who helped relieve my symptoms but didn’t address the deeper root. No one ever mentioned scar tissue, let alone the various forms it can take or how those forms interrelate with other dimensions of health, until I learned anatomy and physiology with Ellen Heed during my Sexological Bodywork certification training.
    - My search led me to integrate Tami Lynn Kent’s teachings on the energetic and creative dimensions of Holistic Pelvic Care, and I go on searching and integrating, because I know what it’s like to stand vulnerable on the edge of emotional, physical, psychological, and spiritual thresholds. I know what it means to be met with the kind of compassion that restores dignity after years of being met short.
    - This path has been one of re-membering, as in making members of myself again — self-ownership, internal authority, and personal boundaries. I am now able to stay present with my suffering, my body, and my pleasure; to take responsibility for my voice and choices; and to relate anew to cultural conditioning, survival strategies, and shame. I have learned to stay humble and to discern when it’s time to receive support. We are not alone. We need each other. We are intimately interdependent with everything on this earth.
    - These embodied inquiries continue to deepen how I move between the dualities of existence. Yes and no, pain and pleasure, shame and dignity, beauty and horror. The whole of life and death.
    - The body is slow, like her soil and her seasons. Transformation and integration are delicate. Processes take time, and they deserve it.
    - This is why I feel empowered to use my experiences as a blueprint for supporting the liberation of pelvises beyond my own.
