## Seki Method

Developed by visionary dancer, choreographer, and teacher Minako Seki, this method emerges from the premise that gravity is the source of movement, the primary lover we offer ourselves to.

Imagine the body suspended by a single thread, passing up through the crown of the head into the cosmos, and down through the center into the earth. Locating that line, the Tan-Den, becomes the anchor for everything else. From there, the body is approached as a bag of water: soft, fluid, sensitive to its own weight. Every step becomes a small fall toward the earth's center, and every fall its own natural bounce back into motion. The dancing body emerges from surrender – releasing effort.

Sense-based exercises loosen inhibition and deepen breath, awakening the connection between mind and body before movement begins. Movement and impulses are translated into visual images that can become blueprints for whatever is being imagined. A daily meditation practice, rooted in mindful walking, sitting, and bowing, cultivates the presence underneath it all, sharpening awareness of one's own patterns so they can be recognized and, when ready, released. Throughout, the method favors raw impulse and imagination over intellectualized movement, closer to instinct than to technique, closer to a child's first relationship to their own body than to anything learned.

This same spirit extends beyond dance into how the body is lived in and cared for day to day, treated as a limb of the earth herself. Food becomes an elixir, a direct source of lightness and vitality, approached through simple questions: how do you want to feel, and what is your body asking for after eating? Eating slowly, chewing fully, and noticing what brings lightness versus heaviness becomes its own practice, held with gratitude for food's journey from nature through human hands. Alongside this sits a lineage of traditional Japanese healing, compresses, herbal baths, and simple home remedies, drawing vitality from nature.

Together, these threads offer one continuous thread: a body moving from its own natural intelligence, relaxed and responsive to gravity and breath, creatively fertile, and nourished in its ongoing relationship with the earth that it hangs from.

## Somatic Sexology – Certified Sexological Bodywork

Sexual health, at its root, is the awareness of being and the capacity to feel alive — to move through the world with ease, spaciousness, and a felt sense of belonging in one's own skin. It's an ongoing practice of returning home to the body as a source of aliveness, creativity, and connection to something larger than ourselves.

Somatic Sex Education approaches this as something to re-member: to gather together the parts of ourselves that are essentially whole — before shame, disconnection, or disruptive experience ask us to forget. The work may include one-way, client-directed touch and is collaborative by nature. A co-created space for exploration and witnessing what wants to surface, where curiosity leads and everything is simply an invitation. There are no expectations nor requirements.

At its core, this work engages with the patterns held in the nervous system, the shapes and habits the body has learned over a lifetime, and offers new pathways through them, toward a fuller, more unguarded expression of one's own aliveness. It becomes a way of opening to life's own ecstatic current, and of reclaiming access to one's power, pleasure, creativity, and the voice or purpose that wants to move through them.

Within this space lives a kind of alchemy: pleasure as a doorway, not an indulgence. For some, it becomes the practice that thrives where talk therapy, affirmation, or seeking outside oneself falls short, a descent into the body's own darkness that turns out to hold its clearest light. Touch, breath, and attention become a way of meeting what's tightly held, inherited ideals, borrowed identities, old armor, and letting it fall away, layer by layer, until what remains feels unmistakably one's own.

### What may bring someone to this work

Every body arrives whole, wired for aliveness, capable of pleasure, desire, and deep feeling. Somewhere along the way, many of us were taught to distrust sensation or natural curiosity. Taught that certain kinds of bodies, certain desires, certain expressions of love or gender or identity, were shameful, dangerous, or simply not allowed.

This may have led to a loss of access to a full range of sensual pleasure. Transitions emerge that ask us to come close to something we may otherwise not be asked to notice. Stress, illness, surgery, medications, gender dysphoria, hormonal changes, birth experiences, trauma both recent and long past, and the inherited weight of repressive cultural conditioning around sexuality, gender, disability, and race are some of the things that may interfere with erotic well-being, sometimes gradually, sometimes all at once.

You are not alone if you encounter challenges to relax, stay present, or feel arousal in your body. If you freeze, zone out, or disconnect during intimacy. If touch that once felt good no longer lands the same way, or doesn't land at all. If pleasure feels elusive, distant, numb, overwhelming, or strangled by shame.

The body responds to time, safety, and attuned attention. This work may be showing the body, patiently and repeatedly, that what it once organized around is no longer present. Sometimes it's offering the opportunity to discover what pleasure actually feels like, for the first time, without shame as a voiceover. Sometimes it's learning to distinguish the body's own voice from every voice that has ever lied to it that it wasn't sacred

A body that has reclaimed its own aliveness has more to offer everyone around it. More presence, more capacity, more staying power for whatever life-work it's part of.

## Holistic Pelvic Care™

Holistic Pelvic Care™ approaches the pelvic bowl as a root, where creative, sexual, and life force energy originates. An altar for tending with attention, intention, and nourishment for all its wild creative potential.

Think of the pelvic bowl like the base of a plant, hidden beneath the soil. Even when everything above ground looks fine, compressed roots slow growth throughout the whole plant. Restoring flow at the root changes what's possible elsewhere.

Developed by Tami Lynn Kent, a pioneering Pelvic Floor Physical Therapist, this work addresses the energetic and physical fields of the pelvic bowl. It can work at purely the energetic level with great efficacy. When working at the physical level, there is use of a gentle internal myofascial technique paired with breath and visualization to bring awareness and circulation back to tissues.

The intention is to restore proprioception and ease imbalance — supporting menstrual and sexual health, conception and postpartum recovery, and healing after trauma, loss, or surgery. It's especially suited to those navigating pelvic pain, preparing for or recovering from birth, moving through menopause, or simply seeking a embodied sense of this creative and fertile part of themselves.

## Zen Shiatsu Bodywork

Beyond massage, Zen Shiatsu engages the meridians, the subtle pathways beneath the muscular layer that carry the vital energy – qi – connecting our organs, nervous system, and inner state. Rather than manipulating tissue, I offer sustained, grounded pressure and gentle stretches to release where energy may have become stuck, worn, or thin.

A nervous system under chronic stress is like a stream narrowed by debris — still moving, but constricted. Shiatsu clears that channel, giving the system room to settle into its own rhythm. This is especially valuable for trauma and chronic stress. This work builds the nervous system's capacity to hold stillness and safety, so relaxation has somewhere stable to land.

Currently a student in training, with a decade of Zen Buddhist meditation practice centered on gathering attention in the hara and cultivating a quality of open, spacious awareness that shapes the presence and depth of listening I bring to each session.
